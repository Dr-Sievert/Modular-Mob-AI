Reading the map

North is up and east is right, like a Minecraft map. The agent is green, its opponent red, each drawn at the real hitbox width with a health ring. The cone shows where each one faces; the agent's aim ray follows its look direction in 3D and ends where it meets the ground (×) or the opponent's hitbox (ring). The shadow shifts further south-east the higher a fighter is off the ground.

Projectiles fly in their owner's colour with a short trail, their shadow showing how high they are. An arrow that hit a block stays where it landed; a ring marks where each flight ended. On the timeline, a tick is a shot, a triangle a hit on a fighter, and a circle a hit on a block.

The 3D view shows the same moment. Served by scripts\viewer.ps1 with a Minecraft jar in the Gradle cache, the fighters are their Minecraft mobs, textures read from your own jar: the head follows the look, the body lags towards where the mob walks, the arms swing on attacks, a mob flashes red when hurt and falls over at 0 health. Otherwise they are their hitboxes, with a visor at eye height; either way name and health float above them. The ground is the real blocks of the site, recorded when the fight started: trees with open ground under their leaves, plants, vines and roots, water with its bed. Served with a Minecraft jar every block has its own texture, otherwise its map colour. A fighter hidden behind blocks shows through them as a see-through silhouette.

Served by scripts\viewer.ps1, replays can be deleted from the list: the bin on a replay or a run, or tick replays (Shift-click for a range, the box on a run for all of it) and delete them together. It always asks first.

Numbers

Aim error is the angle between the agent's look direction (from its eyes) and the direction to the centre of the opponent's hitbox. Yaw "right" means the agent looks to the right of the opponent; pitch "low" means below it. On target means the look ray passes through the opponent's hitbox.

Actions are what the brain asked for before the game applied its limits. Buttons count as held at 0.5 or more. aimYaw and aimPitch are the turn this tick as a fraction of 60°.
